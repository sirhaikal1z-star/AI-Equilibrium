LEARN

Core Concepts

Build the foundation before moving into calculations and laboratory simulations.

01

Arrhenius Acids & Bases

An Arrhenius acid increases the concentration of hydrogen ions in aqueous solution.

HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻

An Arrhenius base increases the concentration of hydroxide ions in aqueous solution.

NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻
02

Brønsted–Lowry Theory

A Brønsted–Lowry acid is a proton donor, while a Brønsted–Lowry base is a proton acceptor.

NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻

In this reaction, NH₃ accepts a proton and H₂O donates a proton.

03

Conjugate Acid–Base Pairs

A conjugate acid–base pair differs by one proton, H⁺.

HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻
HA ⇌ A⁻

HA is the acid and A⁻ is its conjugate base.

04

Strong & Weak Acids

Strong acids ionise essentially completely in aqueous solution.

HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻

Weak acids establish an equilibrium between the undissociated acid and its ions.

CH₃COOH ⇌ H⁺ + CH₃COO⁻
05

Strong & Weak Bases

Strong bases dissociate extensively in water, while weak bases establish an equilibrium.

NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻
06

Acid Dissociation Constant

The acid dissociation constant describes the equilibrium position of a weak acid.

Kₐ = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA]

A larger Kₐ generally indicates a stronger weak acid.
